## Part 1: Project Brief

### 1. Client Brief

* **Context:** A public transport organization operating hundreds of buses with thousands of passengers and staff members faces an invisible barrier between driving staff (who are on the road) and office staff (who plan and manage operations).


* **Core Problem:** Communication is often fragmented, traditional, and sensitive to time constraints, leading to a gap where drivers do not feel heard, innovative and practical solutions are lacking, and valuable operational feedback is lost.


* **Objective:** Find solutions to encourage structural interaction between driving and office staff to bridge this operational divide.


* **Scope:** Digital tools, platforms, or workflows that enable accessible, low-barrier, two-way communication and structural feedback mechanisms between mobile personnel and stationary operations teams.



### 2. Requirement

* **Target Improvements:**
* Build a bridge between driving staff and office/management personnel.


* Improve mutual understanding, feedback loops, and operational engagement.


* Ensure high accessibility for mobile workers without adding heavy administrative burdens.




* **In-Scope Outputs:** Scalable digital tools, communication platforms, or application concepts that foster daily engagement and structural feedback.


* **Exclusions:**
* Heavy enterprise software that requires complicated downloads or disrupts mobile workflows.


* Solutions that lack simple, low-threshold authentication or demand high-risk infrastructure investments.



### 3. Investigation

* **Domain Exploration:** Analyze organizational dynamics between mobile field staff and stationary office personnel in transportation or shift-based industries.
* **Data & Technical Inquiry:** Examine lightweight communication architectures (e.g., mobile-first web apps, progressive web apps, or integrated messaging interfaces) that accommodate variable work schedules and shift work.

### 4. Issue

* **Core Tensions:** Balancing the need for structured corporate communication with the reality that drivers have limited time, dynamic schedules, and lack constant access to traditional desktop environments.

### 5. Contribution

* **Student Output:** Design an independent conceptual framework, functional prototype, or engagement strategy that systematically captures driver feedback and integrates it into office operations.

### 6. Review

* **Evaluation Criteria:** Assess proposed solutions on usability for non-desk workers, adoption feasibility, privacy, and tangible impact on organizational cohesion.

### 7. Reflection

* **Process Analysis:** Critically evaluate how digital tools can successfully overcome organizational silos between distributed field workers and central management.

### 8. Insight

* **Transferable Learning:** Extract broader principles regarding engagement design and communication architecture for workforce management in mobile-heavy industries.

## Part 2: Example Questions & Answers

*The following anonymized Q&A log serves as an example of how stakeholders probe assumptions, clarify constraints, and refine requirements during a real project lifecycle:*

* **Q: Is an app required, or is a web page that can be accessed via mobile and desktop also fine?**
* *A:* We do not want an app that needs to be downloaded from an app store; it is important that our employees can find it via an accessible interface without annoying installation hurdles.




* **Q: How will the pilot project be structured if selected?**
* *A:* Selected solutions will move into a pilot phase of roughly 3 months to test viability with a localized group of drivers and office staff.




* **Q: What constitutes success for structural interaction?**
* *A:* Success is measured by regular, low-friction touchpoints where insights flow seamlessly from the road back to planners and managers, leading to visible changes in operational practices.
