## Part 1: Project Brief

### 1. Client Brief

* **Context:** In a crisis or crisis-sensitive county, the process of information dissemination is extremely intense, involving changing channels, transforming media, multi-channel communication strategies, and high-pressure coordination with emergency partners, citizens, and media.


* **Core Problem:** Crisis management teams are under high pressure, facing a flood of data, manual reporting, and information overload while trying to keep the public and partners informed.


* **Objective:** Find smart AI support solutions for communication staff to reduce cognitive load and enhance communication during incidents.


* **Scope:** AI tools, automated synthesis pipelines, or digital support frameworks that assist crisis management teams in processing data, drafting messaging, coordinating channels, and maintaining situational awareness.



### 2. Requirement

* **Target Improvements:**
* Support crisis communication teams with automated processing, information retrieval, and synthesis.


* Streamline the generation of consistent multi-channel communication (social media, press releases, internal updates).


* Provide reliable, fast insights under high-pressure disaster conditions without introducing hallucination risks.




* **In-Scope Outputs:** Proof-of-concepts, AI agent frameworks, or digital tools designed to assist communication staff in crisis management environments.


* **Exclusions:**
* Standard public-facing chatbot solutions that are not tailored to internal crisis coordination and communication operations.


* High-risk automated systems that publish messages without human oversight or control.





### 3. Investigation

* **Domain Exploration:** Analyze crisis communication workflows, emergency response coordination structures, and the operational bottlenecks faced by public information officers during disasters.
* **Data & Technical Inquiry:** Examine how natural language processing, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and content classification can safely aggregate incident data and assist in drafting clear, verified updates.

### 4. Issue

* **Core Tensions:** Balancing the urgent need for speed and automated efficiency in a crisis with the absolute requirement for accuracy, accountability, and safety in public messaging.

### 5. Contribution

* **Student Output:** Develop an independent conceptual design, architectural prototype, or functional tool that assists communication teams in processing crisis data and generating coordinated responses.

### 6. Review

* **Evaluation Criteria:** Assess the proposed solution on reliability under pressure, mitigation of misinformation/hallucination, ease of integration into existing crisis workflows, and user safety.

### 7. Reflection

* **Process Analysis:** Critically evaluate how artificial intelligence can safely augment human decision-making and communication under extreme time constraints and high-stress conditions.

### 8. Insight

* **Transferable Learning:** Extract broader principles regarding human-AI teaming, trust, and safety-critical automation in high-stakes operational environments.

## Part 2: Example Questions & Answers

*The following placeholder represents how questions and clarifications are structured for this type of challenge:*

* **Q: Does the AI need to autonomously publish messages to the public?**
* *A:* No, the focus is entirely on *supporting* communication staff internally—reducing their workload, drafting options, and managing information streams. Final verification and publishing remain strictly human-controlled.




* **Q: What kind of data sources will the system need to process?**
* *A:* Incoming reports from emergency services, social media feeds, media monitoring, and internal operational updates.
