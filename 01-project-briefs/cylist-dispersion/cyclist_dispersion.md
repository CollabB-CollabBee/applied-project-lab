## Part 1: Project Brief

### 1. Client Brief

* **Context:** In historic city centers, the use of bicycles is growing rapidly, making streets increasingly busy and putting pressure on public space, accessibility, and traffic safety, particularly for vulnerable road users.


* **Core Problem:** Certain routes experience heavy bicycle crowding during peak hours, creating friction, safety hazards, and congestion that traditional traffic measures cannot adequately solve.


* **Objective:** Find smart, dynamic solutions to reduce bicycle crowds on specific routes in the city center.


* **Scope:** Dynamic routing concepts, smart guidance systems, digital nudging tools, or behavioral interventions that influence cyclist flows and distribute traffic more evenly across alternative paths.



### 2. Requirement

* **Target Improvements:**
* Real-time insights into bicycle flows and crowd density on specific routes.


* Effective nudging or routing mechanisms that encourage cyclists to choose alternative routes.


* Maintain pedestrian safety and improve overall traffic flow without heavily altering physical infrastructure.




* **In-Scope Outputs:** Proof-of-concepts, smart digital applications, or dynamic guidance systems aimed at mitigating urban bicycle congestion.


* **Exclusions:**
* Static traffic signs or generic navigation apps that do not feature dynamic crowd management or local route adaptation.


* Solutions that require high-risk investments in new physical infrastructure or heavy structural road changes.





### 3. Investigation

* **Domain Exploration:** Analyze urban cycling behavior, peak-hour traffic bottlenecks, psychological triggers for route choice, and municipal spatial planning constraints.
* **Data & Technical Inquiry:** Examine how real-time sensor data, municipal traffic feeds, or mobile software interfaces can dynamically communicate alternative pathways to active cyclists.

### 4. Issue

* **Core Tensions:** Balancing the freedom of individual cyclists to choose their route with the collective municipal need to manage density, prevent bottlenecks, and safeguard vulnerable road users in historic spaces.

### 5. Contribution

* **Student Output:** Develop an independent conceptual design, behavioral framework, or digital prototype that addresses the reduction and redistribution of bicycle traffic on congested routes.

### 6. Review

* **Evaluation Criteria:** Assess proposed solutions based on technical feasibility, effectiveness in shifting behavior, scalability for urban environments, and impact on safety.

### 7. Reflection

* **Process Analysis:** Critically evaluate how digital nudging and dynamic data systems can influence real-time crowd dynamics in complex public spaces.

### 8. Insight

* **Transferable Learning:** Extract broader principles regarding behavioral design, spatial management, and real-time crowd control in urban mobility networks.
