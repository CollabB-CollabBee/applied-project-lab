# applied-project-lab
Applied Project Lab is a structured environment for learning through doing. Participants contribute to real projects, review peers, reflect on mistakes, and extract transferable insights. The goal is not just building software, but learning how to navigate project reality.



* Applied Project Lab is a contribution-based learning framework.
* The objective is not simply to learn tools.
* The objective is to learn how to participate in real projects.
* Participants contribute to projects, review the work of others, reflect on mistakes, and extract transferable insights.
* Learning occurs through contribution, review, reflection, and iteration.

## Goals:
* we do not create perfect solutions.
* we develop great judgments.

## What Students Bring In

A student's project should not be treated as a practice project.

**It should be treated as a first-class project.**

This distinction is important.

Applied Project Lab is not built around a single product or repository.

Rather than saying:

> "Come help on my project."

we are saying:

> "Bring your own project and apply the same framework."

Participants can use existing projects, startup ideas, community initiatives, research projects, or other real-world work as the foundation for their learning journey.

The framework remains the same. The project context may differ.

## Certification Origin

Project work takes place within the participant's own project environment.

Certification and achievement tracking are managed through CollabB-CollabBee, which acts as an independent third party.

This separation helps ensure that recognition is based on demonstrated contribution, review, reflection, and learning rather than participation in any specific project.

## Ethical Project Guidelines

Participants are welcome to bring their own projects into Applied Project Lab.

Projects should align with basic ethical, legal, and educational principles. The purpose of the framework is to help participants develop professional judgment through constructive, real-world work.

Projects will not be accepted if their primary purpose is to:

- Cause harm to individuals, organisations, or communities.
- Create, distribute, or facilitate malware, viruses, ransomware, or other malicious software.
- Enable unauthorised access to systems, networks, or data.
- Support fraud, deception, harassment, or illegal activities.
- Violate privacy, intellectual property, or applicable laws.
- Promote harmful, abusive, or discriminatory behaviour.

When uncertainty exists, project suitability will be reviewed on a case-by-case basis.

Applied Project Lab reserves the right to decline projects that conflict with these principles.

The objective is not only to build things, but to develop the responsibility, judgment, and professional conduct required to contribute positively to real projects and society.
